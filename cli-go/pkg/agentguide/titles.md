# Suggesting a title for a post

A post goes out signed in the owner's name, and its title is what most readers see first: in their
network's lists, in links, in search. Help the owner pick one; never choose it for them.

1. **Read the whole draft first.** Note its first heading, if it has one, and what the post actually
   argues or tells, which is not always what the heading says.
2. **Keep the owner's title if it works.** Say so in a line, and move on. Suggest changes only when a
   title is vague, misleading, much longer than it needs to be, or missing.
3. **Offer two or three options**, each a line, with a few words on what each one stresses. Plain and
   specific beats clever: a reader should know what the post is about from the title alone.
4. **Write in the owner's voice.** Match how they title their other posts (their recent posts are a fair
   guide: casing, length, whether they use questions or colons).
5. **Don't repeat the first sentence.** A title that is just the opening words of the body reads twice on
   the page. Say what the post is about instead.
6. **Change the title only.** Never edit the body while suggesting a title, unless the owner asks.
7. **The owner decides.** Publish only when they say the post is ready, with the title they chose.
