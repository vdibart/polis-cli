# In Defense of Bless

---

**bless** · /blɛs/ · *verb*

1. *Religion.* To confer favor or approval; to acknowledge the good in those under one's care. — *"the priest blessed the congregation"*
2. *Colloquial.* To wish well; to regard with gratitude or affection. — *"bless you"; "I'm blessed"; "bless this mess"*
3. *Gaming.* To grant a boon or buff; to upgrade or strengthen a character's traits or inventory. — *receiving a god's blessing in* Hades
4. *Programming (Perl).* To convert a loose, anonymous, throwaway reference into a first-class object.  — `bless $ref, $class`

---

"Why would you use such a potentially devisive name instead of just calling it
'approve' or 'endorse' like other platforms?"

Fair question.  We get it. For some people the word lands wrong — and it's almost
always definition 1 setting that tone: the religious context, the sense of someone
set above you, handing down approval from on high.  But "blessing" in polis carries
a unique meaning that highlights why polis is different, and we think once you
understand that you'll see why the word fits perfectly.

When you bless a comment in polis, the effect is closer to the last three
definitions than the first. Say you comment on alice.polis.pub's article. That
comment lives on *your* server — Alice can't delete it, and she didn't have to
let you write it. What she can do is *bless* it: promote it from a loose
reference floating on your server into a first-class part of her conversation,
shown alongside her post, in her stream, in front of her followers. Gamers
already know the shape of this — a blessing is a boon, a buff; it only ever adds
power, never takes it away. A promotion and a power-up, not a sacrament.

And because it only ever adds, no platform could use the word honestly. On a
platform, speech and reach are the same lever — an algorithm decides how far you
travel, and "bless" would just be a fancy word for permission to be heard,
handed down by whoever owns the pipes. In polis it can't mean that, because your
voice was free before anyone blessed anything.

Here's where we stop being clever. We didn't pick "bless" only for its Perl
precision or its gamer's swagger. We picked it because the second definition is
true too — choosing to carry someone's words to your own people genuinely is an
act of goodwill, a quiet *I'm glad you said this*. And the first definition,
softened to what it really means — acknowledging the good in what someone else
made — turns out to be true here as well. Only the author can do it, and it says
something real about what they think belongs in their conversation. Anyone can
*like* a comment. Only Alice can bless one.

So your first instinct wasn't wrong. The word carries all of that — a promotion,
a buff, a kindness, an acknowledgment of something good — all at once, and all
true here.

You saw it as one thing. It turned out to be several. That's not a trick; it's a
word doing several honest jobs that happen to be the same job in polis: a precise
operation, performed by a person, as an act of goodwill.

We're not asking you to love the word, or even to use it. We're asking you to let
the other definitions sit beside the first — because the openness that takes is
the same openness it takes to see what polis is for.
